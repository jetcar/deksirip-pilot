# Comparative Analogs and Comparison Boundaries

The Chinese examples below demonstrate separate institutions or a pilot method;
they do not show that the full DEKSIRIP model has already been implemented.

| Proposal element | Observable analogue | What can be claimed | What cannot be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot-based rule change | Special economic zones and pilot free-trade zones | Bounded territories can test rules before possible expansion | That a pilot zone already tests democratic allocation of common surplus |
| Common assets and local income | Rural collective enterprises and collective dividends | Collective assets can generate local income and payments to members | That this equals an equal dividend for all residents or prevents insider control |
| Basic services and lower gaps | Zhejiang Common Prosperity Demonstration Zone | Public services and inequality measures can be coordinated at large scale | That basic provision is already separated from employment and private ownership |
| Natural-asset register and governance | Chinese reforms of registration, use rights and natural-resource revenue | Inventory, separation of ownership and use, and revenue/accountability controls are possible | That residents directly govern the entire resource portfolio |
| Strategic reserves | State and local food, energy and medical reserves | Advance provisioning of critical inputs is an established institution | That reserve targets are set through open resident participation |
| Transparent cost information | Price, market and pilot-zone reforms | Prices and rules can act as signals and be tested under different regimes | That coordination without private rent as the main incentive is already proven |

## Conclusion

The strongest defensible claim is that China has experience with staged pilots,
collective assets, public services and resource governance. The research question
is not to reinvent every element, but to test a bounded combination with
pre-registered evaluation, transparency and a right of exit.
