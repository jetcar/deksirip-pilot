# Working Definitions and Main Objections

This document fixes the meaning of key terms before institutions and technology
are discussed.

## 1. Working definitions

| Term | Meaning in the proposal |
|---|---|
| **Common resource** | An asset or flow whose access rules are set by a public or collective mandate rather than one private owner. It does not mean unlimited access for everyone. |
| **Basic good** | A minimum amount of a life-essential good recognised as a pilot participant’s right. It is not a promise of unlimited consumption. |
| **Surplus** | Resources remaining after basic obligations, capacity maintenance, reserves and ecological limits. It must be counted physically and financially. |
| **Social dividend** | An equal or pre-agreed share of the distributable result of common assets. It is a claim on the result, not personal ownership of the asset. |
| **Transparency** | The ability to verify data sources, rules, calculations, decisions and outcomes. It is not publication of all personal information. |
| **Participation** | The ability to influence decisions affecting common resources within law, available balances and procedure. It does not require voting on every operation. |
| **Cost information** | Direct and indirect inputs, capacity limits, scarcity, opportunity cost and material external damage. It informs decisions; it is not an automatic fair-price formula. |
| **Pilot** | A bounded experiment with voluntary participation, comparison, limited territory, time, resources and authority, and a right to stop. |

## 2. Main objections

| Objection | Model response | What the pilot must test |
|---|---|---|
| People will use the system without helping maintain it | A basic right does not remove contribution, care and accountability rules; these must be separate from political punishment | Whether participation deficits emerge or mutual support increases |
| Reliable information about all resources is impossible | The model does not require perfect information, but requires data quality and uncertainty to be visible | Whether the gap between reported and actual resources decreases |
| A majority may take rights from a minority | Basic rights and appeal must not depend on the current vote; decisions remain bounded by law and procedure | Whether vulnerable groups are protected and appeals work |
| Voting will be slow and paralyse production | Daily work remains with mandate-limited executors; collective decisions concern goals and major changes | Decision time and any effect on output |
| Transparency will become surveillance | Aggregates and proofs are public, not unnecessary personal histories | Whether decisions can be checked without exposing private life |
| AI will amplify manipulation | AI is an explanatory, auditable assistant with no voting or execution power | Whether people detect errors, uncertainty and alternatives better with it |
| Participants will leave for the ordinary market economy | The external economy remains; exit is part of the design, not a failure | Why participants stay or leave and what changes |
| Administration will cost too much | The pilot must count administrative time, audit and support as costs | Whether costs exceed the public result |

## 3. Claim boundary

Three levels must remain separate:

1. **Value:** basic goods and no rent from common assets are desirable.
2. **Hypothesis:** transparent matching of needs and resources improves outcomes.
3. **Fact:** a specific pilot produced a measured change against a pre-defined
   indicator.

The first two levels must not be presented as the third.
