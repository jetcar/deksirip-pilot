# Minimum Requirements for a Zone: What Is Needed, How Much, and Why

Draft. Describes the minimum conditions under which the model can be tested on
an existing territory, and explains every number: why not less and why not more.

The document does not claim that the model works. It claims that at these values
a test is meaningful at all, and below them it is not.

## 1. Three pillars the calculation rests on

Everything else follows from them.

**First: the measured base.** Sweden 2025 — 765.4 hours worked per resident per
year (8.1172 billion hours for 10.6 million people). This is the upper bound of
labour requirements under the present organisation, not the requirement itself:
inside it sit exports, the administrative overhead and work performed under
compulsion.

**Second: labour is counted in tasks, not professions.** What disappears is not
the cashier but the operation "ring up the purchase". Restocking, error
handling, returns and helping people remain. This rule comes from S3, and it is
also the reason present employment cannot serve as an estimate.

**Third: automation is an investment, not a fund.** An hour spent on automation
is justified only if it retires more than an hour of future labour. Otherwise it
is consumption of time under the guise of work — which is exactly what the
growth of documentation looks like.

## 2. Eight requirements

| Requirement | Value | Why not less | Why not more |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Population | 10,000–50,000 | below 10,000 there are too few people for specialists and for review, and care absorbs all the freed time | above 50,000 the zone grows its own bureaucracy, and proximity and the right of exit lose meaning |
| Of working age | 50–60% | below, care and production do not balance | above, the demography is unrealistic |
| Hours per resident per year | 200–400 | care, repair and exception handling do not compress below this | above 450 the main non-monetary gain disappears — time for care and for oneself |
| Deep specialists | 1–2% of the population | below, there is no review, no second opinion, and a single point of failure | more is not needed: review does not parallelise, and the surplus drifts into meta-work |
| Process designers | 5–10% | below, automation stays cosmetic and does not change flows | — |
| AI-equipped operators | 40–60% | below, most processes have nobody to run them from documentation | — |
| External turnover | 10–20% of output | below, there is nothing to pay for imported automation, medicines and components | above, the zone becomes an export colony and loses autonomy |
| Meta-work | ≤ 30% of hours | — | above, documentation starts feeding itself, which is the first sign of failure |

## 3. Where the 200–400 hours come from

Not by summing three guesses, but as a residual. One figure is measured;
everything else is subtracted from it and labelled.

| Component | Hours per resident | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| Total worked, S0 | 765 | measured (Sweden 2025) |
| minus export labour: paying for imports | 180–240 | estimate from the share of exports in hours |
| minus organisational overhead: administration, finance, legal, sales, compliance | 110–170 | estimate, typical range 15–25% of employment |
| **= the basket at present technology** | **355–475**, centre ≈ 425 | **the key unknown: requires calculation, not belief** |
| Total S0 | 765 | reconciles by construction |

Then S3:

- mature automation removes 40–60% of the basket hours → 140–285;
- maintaining the automation itself adds 10% → 155–315;
- export labour to pay for imports remains, but less: 40–90.

**Result: 195–405 hours per resident per year, centre around 300.** At 55% of
working age that is **7–14 hours per week per working-age person**, around 11 in
the middle.

The spread is honest, and it narrows in two ways: by calculating the basket (425
is the weakest number in this document) and by deciding how much the zone sells
outward. The second is an internal political decision, not a technical constant,
which is why it cannot be a single number.

## 4. Where the freed time goes

A zone of 20,000 people, with shares typical for ageing Europe.

| Load | Who | Hours per year | Full-time equivalents |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Elderly 65+ | 4,000 people, of whom 25% need daily help, 1.5 h/day | 548,000 | 275 |
| Children | 3,600 people, institutional part 0.5 h/day | 657,000 | 330 |
| Repair and upkeep of the housing stock | all residential and non-residential floor area | 300,000–450,000 | 150–225 |
| Training and retraining | 20% of the population at 40 h/year | 160,000 | 80 |
| Total | | 1.7–1.8 m | 835–910 |

That is 4–4.5% of the population and **7–8% of the working-age**. At 765 hours
per resident this load is invisible; at 250 hours it becomes the main item — and
that is the answer to the care objection.

The mechanism is simple: care does not need new technicians, it needs time.
Forty hours a week leaves none of it; eleven leaves some. The same reasoning
implies that the model **cannot** start in a zone with a high share of elderly
and a low share of working age: there the freed time goes entirely into care,
and nothing remains for automation.

## 5. The share of the skilled: three tiers

Not "the percentage of educated people", but three distinct functions that
cannot substitute for one another.

| Tier | What they do | Share | Why this number |
|---|---|---:|---|
| AI-equipped operator | runs a process from its documentation, notices and describes exceptions | 40–60% | this is most people's work; AI lowers the entry threshold but does not remove responsibility |
| Process designer | redesigns a whole flow rather than a single step | 5–10% | needed in every domain; below that, automation stays cosmetic |
| Deep specialist | checks the output of the AI and of the designer, and owns the refusal | 1–2% | review does not parallelise: one per domain, plus a reserve for replacement |

The key point: **AI raises the lower tier and barely moves the upper one.** A
householder with AI really does cover most operator tasks — and precisely for
that reason the bottleneck moves upward: somebody has to check that the
generated process does not break the system. One percent of deep specialists is
not an elite, it is insurance.

A practical consequence: a zone without that percentage can start, but it cannot
automate itself. It will buy automation from outside, which means paying for it
with exports, which means working more. This is testable, and it should be
counted before the start.

## 6. Governance: numbers constrain, people decide

The numbers in this document are not decisions, they are a frame. Nothing here
says what to automate first, where the surplus should go, how many hours to work
or what to sell outward. The zone decides that.

But there is a thin layer that cannot be re-voted every time: the rules of
rule-making. If a majority can redefine the procedure to suit the current
interest, the minority has no reason to observe it, and the whole construction
rests on good faith. So what is fixed before the start is not the budget but the
procedure:

1. Who is a member, how membership is gained and how it is lost.
2. What is guaranteed unconditionally — and that it cannot be taken from a
   particular person by a majority vote.
3. How decisions are made: proposal, published trade-off, vote or delegated
   mandate, revocation.
4. How a minority appeals, and to whom.
5. How a mandate is time-limited and how it is revoked.
6. The right of exit — and what a person takes with them.

Everything else is ordinary politics: budget, projects, automation, investment,
hours, exports. One rule applies: **a decision without a published reason is
void.** That is what distinguishes the model from administrative allocation, and
it is also what makes it testable.

## 7. Refusal to participate

The problem does not exist on its own; it exists because the model delivers an
unconditional base and an equal dividend at the same time. Separating those two
is the design choice.

| Scheme | Unconditional | Depends on participation | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Everything unconditional and equal | base and dividend | nothing | refusal is rational at the margin; held together only by the norm |
| B. Base unconditional, dividend by contribution | base | dividend | requires defining contribution; risk of accounting bureaucracy |
| C. All equal, but a minimum contribution set by vote | base | the rule on the minimum | the rule can be tightened into exclusion |
| D. Transparency without formal sanctions | everything | nothing | punishes invisible work: care, illness, age |
| E. Dividend proportional to hours | base | dividend | turns time back into money |

The evidence on unconditional income: labour supply falls by 2–8%, and the fall
concentrates among mothers of small children and students, not among "the lazy".
But those pilots are time-limited and externally funded: the effect of "this
will end someday" disappears, and the effect of "the neighbours are watching"
appears. Those figures cannot be transferred to a permanent community, and that
should be said plainly.

What works here is not morality but Ostrom's principles for common-pool
resources: boundaries, rules congruent with local conditions, participation of
those the rules affect, monitoring by the users themselves, and **graduated
sanctions** — starting with a warning rather than expulsion, with a conflict
resolution mechanism. No external police is required.

And the arithmetic that matters more than morality. The base is about 200 hours
out of 300 per resident. If 5% refuse, the zone loses about 3% of output:
tolerable. If 30% refuse, it loses a fifth, and the model does not hold. So the
question is not "how to punish" but "does the norm survive high tolerance". That
is a dynamic, and it can be measured better than it can be argued.

### Earnings from outside

This is not a new risk but an unmade decision. A resident who works for an
external employer lives cheaply inside and is paid outside. Leaving is not
attractive — living inside is cheaper than outside — so the situation will not
resolve itself and has to be counted.

**The guarantee everything rests on:** external money buys nothing inside.
Internal prices are not market prices and allocation follows rules, so external
income does not convert into privilege. That is precisely what keeps inequality
outside, and it must be protected as a constitutional provision rather than as
ordinary practice.

| Option | What they receive | What it creates | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base only, no dividend | the base | two categories: members and residents | the base is two-thirds of output, so the subsidy remains |
| A share of external earnings into the common pool | base and dividend | external earnings become their export, symmetrical with a company | declarations must be checked; evasion moves to cash |
| Full dividend, external earnings counted as contribution | everything | a simple accounting scheme | internal work loses its advantage, and the zone drifts into a dormitory for external employers |

**The arithmetic.** The base is about 200 hours out of 300 per resident, that is
two-thirds of output. If 10% of residents live this way, the zone loses about 7%
of output; if 20%, about 13%. This is no longer a question of principle but a
number that can be decided by vote and revisited.

**Where the freed share goes.** If the external earner's dividend is not paid,
it has two destinations: redistribute it among the others (every payment rises)
or leave it in the commons for automation. That is a separate decision with a
visible price, and it must not be hidden inside the first one.

**How it is regulated.** Not by a new agency. Revenue is declared, the border
margin goes into the common pool, and the transparency layer catches
discrepancies: undeclared income shows up as a mismatch between lifestyle and
participation. The sanction is the same as everywhere: graduated, not expulsion.

**A testable prediction.** If the share of those living on the base without
internal participation exceeds 15% and no rule on contribution has been adopted,
the zone turns into a dormitory for external employers: internal hours fall while
the base does not shrink. This is fixed as a criterion before the start, not
argued about afterwards.

## 8. Excess consumption and shortage

Two different cases that are usually conflated.

**Real scarcity.** The mechanism already exists in the kit: published stocks, an
access rule, priorities, staged delivery, and an honest "there is not that
much". Rationing by rule, not by price. It is checked by the share of needs met
and by the number of decisions revisited.

**Accumulation out of distrust.** People stockpile not from greed but when they
are unsure of supply. The answer is reserves and visibility: if it is visible
that there is enough and that the stock is stable, accumulation falls. This is
the strongest argument for the transparency layer, and it is testable — compare
household stocks with published reserves and without them.

**Capture of capacity.** One family took the large flat, one workshop took the
energy. What works here is not price but a rule by need — published and
appealable.

## 9. Oversight: who watches, and who watches the watchers

Crime does not disappear. Its composition changes, and that matters more than
its level.

| What happens | Why | Consequence for oversight |
|---|---|---|
| Theft from strangers, street crime | falls: the base is guaranteed, inequality is compressed, anonymity is low | less patrolling, more trust |
| Violence | depends weakly on economics: alcohol, crowding, mental health | remains a matter of medicine and density, not police |
| Abuse of common assets | rises as a share: the temptation is shared while control is weaker than under a private owner | the main object of oversight |

The last row matters more than the others. In a commons the most likely crime
is not theft from a neighbour but a gap between the records and the facts,
favouritism in allocation, and procurement from friends. That is precisely what
the kit already measures: the gap between the register and a physical count, and
the number of decisions reversed by audit. The kit turns out to be not only an
allocation instrument but a detector of abuse — and that should be used rather
than rediscovered.

### Four constructions and the price of each

| Construction | Strength | Price |
|---|---|---|
| The group decides about a particular person | local knowledge beats a central rule | tyranny of the proximate: the judge sits inside the group, interests overlap |
| Contributing a share to oversight services | oversight is paid for by those who want it | free riding; the service starts finding violations to justify its budget |
| Personal participation | cheap, precise local information, strengthens the norm | works for the visible, fails for the invisible: procurement, safety |
| External audit on contract | independence | more expensive, and arrives with its own interests |

Conclusion: the oversight budget is approved by vote rather than collected
voluntarily — otherwise free riding. Checks are triggered by a claim rather than
maintained as a standing service — otherwise there is a demand for violations.
The auditor rotates, the report is published and is itself reviewed — otherwise
audit becomes an industry.

### Three risks that must be named before the start

**Tyranny of the proximate.** A group knows the person well and is poor at
impartiality. So the ladder of measures is fixed in advance, its application is
appealable and time-limited, and a decision about a person is published with its
reason.

**Money must not buy a vote.** If a contribution to oversight buys oversight of
whatever matters to the contributor, money has returned to influence through the
back door.

**Judge of the dissenter.** Where everyone evaluates everyone's contribution,
the person who spends time criticising the plan becomes a candidate for being an
idler. This is the most serious political risk of the model. The defence is not
goodwill but procedure: contribution is defined in advance and as a list, not at
the group's discretion.

## 10. Transparency and AI: what replaces oversight, and what does not

The idea: nobody needs to watch continuously. It is enough that anyone can check
after the fact, and that a machine checks continuously. This moves oversight
from active to **latent** — which is how audit works in practice: the deterrent
is not the check but its possibility.

What this genuinely delivers:

- the cost of reading records falls to almost nothing, and mismatches become
  visible without a specialist;
- a mistake leaves a trace, so accountability is deferred but not lost;
- checks can be targeted: one line, one payment, one question.

Four rules without which this becomes decoration.

**1. No record without an external counterpart.** A register written by those
who spend is a presentation, not accounting. A record is accepted only if it has
an independent twin: a bank statement, a meter reading, a supplier invoice, a
physical count, a timestamp. A line without a twin is an alarm. The log is
append-only and dated; an edited record is an event, not a correction.

**2. Few invariants, but checked always.** "Every payment has a delivery."
"Every promise has a result." "Every reserve has an expiry." "Every expenditure
has a decision." A hundred machine-checkable invariants are worth more than full
transparency of everything: nobody will read the full stream of documents, while
an exception is visible.

**3. The auditor and the author are different models.** The AI that writes the
reports cannot be the one that checks them. Separation of duties applies to
models exactly as it applies to people. The checking rules are published and
versioned, and a report must contain not only what was found but also what could
not be checked.

**4. The AI proposes, the human decides.** If the model defines what counts as
an anomaly and what counts as an explanation, it becomes a judge. Its reasoning
must be checkable, and the decision must rest with people.

An honest correction to "it is impossible to deceive everyone for long". True,
but not automatic. Prominent cases lasted for years not because there were no
traces but because nobody looked, or because the one who looked had an interest.
Transparency shortens the **duration** of deception; it does not make it
impossible, and it works only where there is somebody to file a claim.

The example closest to yours — a building association. Vote → budget line →
payment → delivery → register entry. Four artefacts. You do not need to know why
the money went; you need the absence of any of the four to raise an alarm. That
is the difference between "I trust the board" and "I do not need to understand,
because the system will do it for me".

## 11. Three levers instead of one

A zone has no single "fairness" dial. It has three, and they are linked:

- **cut hours** — a gain now, but less capacity to invest;
- **accumulate surplus** — investment in automation, a gain later;
- **exchange surplus for automation from outside** — faster, but dependent.

It is not only automation that is sold. Any surplus is sold: food, repair,
construction, hospitality, remote hours. The question "what do we sell, and at
what cost in labour" is an internal political decision with a visible price, and
it is the most interesting part of the model. It is also what makes the model
testable: a decision can be taken wrongly and its consequences seen a year later.

## 12. What counts as failure

Numbers fixed before the start. At least two triggered means the mechanism did
not work in these conditions.

1. Hours per resident have not fallen below 500 within three years.
2. Meta-work has exceeded 30% of hours and is growing for a second year.
3. The share of deep specialists is below 1% and falling.
4. The care load has exceeded the freed time.
5. External turnover is above 30% of output and rising.
6. Hours lost to refusal to participate exceed 10% and are rising for a second
   year.
7. A single person has been left without something essential — the stop is
   immediate, without discussion and without a vote.
8. Household stocks are growing while public reserves are unchanged: that is
   distrust, not greed.
9. One group holds more than a third of a capacity for longer than a quarter.
10. A measure against a particular person has been applied to someone who
    publicly criticised the plan or a service — this stops the experiment
    regardless of the reasons.
11. The share of those living on the base without internal participation is
    above 15% and no rule on contribution has been adopted.

## 13. Transition

The task: move an existing territory to the model without making it a coup and
without breaking what already works. There is one source of legitimacy — the
referendum. Everything else follows from it.

### Sequence and the principle "add, don't remove"

It cannot be done strictly step by step — but the non-incremental step is always
an addition, not a demolition. The new is placed beside the old and left to
work; the old is removed only when the new has shown it to be redundant. Then
the losers cannot say their voice was taken away: the voice did not go anywhere,
it flowed into a new form.

### Phases

| Phase | What happens | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Instrument and caretaker | the legal act creating the zone, its competences, a caretaker with a fixed term | the zone exists legally, or the transition does not start |
| 1. Baseline | 12 months of measuring the existing systems, physical and financial, independent | a published baseline, no decisions taken |
| 2. Disclosure | standardised categories, an external twin for every record | a real picture; resistance here is data, not a nuisance |
| 3. Mandate referendum | "do we adopt this path" — a supermajority, a concrete question | a mandate to build, not to demolish |
| 4. Parallel launch | e-voting and transparency run beside the old; the executive works under continuous recall | the new is proven in operation |
| 5. Confirmation referendum | on the real mechanism, not the promised one | only now the old begins to retire |
| 6. Retirement and dividend | the representative layer retires through disuse; the dividend begins only now, when the books are real | the model is switched on |

### Rules that hold across all phases

**Voting after transparency, not before.** Voting on a blind system means
ratifying the picture drawn by whoever controls the accounts. Phases 1–2 come
before phase 3 for that reason.

**Constitutional steps by supermajority.** "Who has a voice" is not an ordinary
budget question. It may be changed only by a heightened majority, otherwise the
majority one day abolishes the very possibility of being contested.

**The executive under continuous recall.** Between referendums, "the will of
the people" is interpreted by whoever remains in place. To avoid plebiscitary
Caesarism — an executive plus rare votes and no assembly — the power of recall
must be a standing mechanism, not an event on election day.

**Buyout, not confiscation.** Rent is bought out of future surplus, spread over
decades. To begin with expropriation is to break the model's own rule on day one
and inherit a permanent legal war.

**Sabotage measured by outcome.** Loud sabotage is rare and visible; real
sabotage is quiet and indistinguishable from incompetence. So metrics are
watched — delivery, downtime, discrepancy — not people. A production stop over a
safety defect looks like sabotage, so stops go to an independent referee, not to
a management decision.

**A caretaker is needed, not a leader.** For a leader of a transition, success
is their own obsolescence, so they have no incentive to finish. A caretaker is
defined differently: to run the sequence and end it. The end of their service is
a duty, not a failure, and they are paid for completing it. Phase 0 is the gate:
no instrument and no caretaker — the transition has honestly not begun.

## 14. Comparison and control group

One city proves nothing. A contrast is needed, and it comes from two places,
as in medicine.

**The control group.** Similar cities where the approach is not applied. They
are not decorative: the economy does not stand still, and without a control any
rise or fall is attributed to "the times". The control absorbs the common trend,
and difference-in-differences — (zone after − zone before) − (control after −
control before) — extracts the effect of the approach itself.

**The "before" is already in the records.** This is not a year of waiting and
not cutting a city out of the economy. Nothing is frozen: the city works as it
worked, and it is observed. Data for the last 3–5 years — hours, wages, rents,
water prices, crime, health — already sit in administrative records. They are
read, not awaited.

**Why the control needs a "before".** The assumption that the cities are
similar can only be checked on their pre-intervention dynamics. If the zone and
the control were already diverging before the start, any after-comparison is
noise. The parallel trend is not decoration but a condition without which the
control means nothing.

**Synthetic control instead of a "similar city".** A single hand-picked city is
almost never similar to the zone on every relevant dimension. The modern method
is a synthetic control: a weighted combination of several cities fitted to the
pre-intervention dynamics. That is what is actually used when someone says "a
comparable state".

**Two limitations that honestly cannot be removed.**

- *Self-selection.* The zone chose itself: a city that votes for the model is
  already different — more cohesive, more educated, readier for change. The
  control removes the time trend but not this effect. Matching and sensitivity
  analysis soften it, but do not remove it.
- *One zone.* A single treated city is N=1: the effect estimate is weak by
  definition. The conclusion will be suggestive rather than proven until the
  experiment is repeated in several zones. This should be recorded as an
  expectation, not a flaw.

**What to fix before the start.** What counts as evidence of success and of
failure, which indicators, against which contrast, at what threshold. Otherwise
the result can always be reinterpreted after the fact in one's own favour.

## 15. The zone's finances

### Mandatory and voluntary

Tax is the base plus a cushion. The base is mandatory and covers the upkeep of
the existing stock, the utility networks and the reserve; voluntary collections
go to new projects and improvements. The boundary is the same one already drawn
between basic income and dividend: the base is mandatory, the surplus is
voluntary.

Upkeep of the stock cannot be voluntary, because infrastructure fails not at the
margin but catastrophically — after years of deferred repair, when the need is
finally visible to everyone and costs five times as much. Hence a rule: a
mandatory percentage of replacement value per year. If nobody pays for a new
road, it truly is not needed. If nobody pays for the repair of an existing one,
that is not a signal — it is accumulated failure.

### Revenues

- **Utility networks:** payment for use plus upkeep. The price is cost, without
  rent.
- **Exports:** trade with the outside through a single trading entity; the
  border margin goes to the common pool.
- **The internal tax:** base plus cushion, its size decided by vote on
  transparent cost.

Transparency makes "the necessary minimum" decidable, not computed: it shows how
much things cost, not what is needed. The dispute over how much medicine and
education is "necessary" is politics, and that is normal.

### Pensions

Basic income replaces the pension — simple and logical. One caveat: the income
of the elderly becomes a political variable tied to the common surplus and
revisited every year, rather than a right that is owed to them. In an ageing
zone this is the most sensitive number in the system, and it is settled by how
society treats its old people, not by economics.

### The state: sponsor, counterpart, judge?

The state creates the zone and stays outside as a neighbour: trade and criminal
audit. This is correct, and it is the one part of the project with working
precedents. But three roles cannot be held by the same hands:

- **sponsor** — the state, which creates and funds;
- **judge** — independent evaluation with criteria fixed in advance;
- **beneficiary** — the state, but only on an honest result.

Merging sponsor and judge turns the experiment into a self-report: an interested
party will find a way to "obtain" the desired result — by hidden subsidy, a
favourable site, muted negatives. So the evaluation is done by independents, not
by the state, and the criteria are fixed before the start.

### The external boundary

The interface with the state is a list, not a slogan:

- **stays with the state:** currency, courts, criminal law, defence, the land
  register, customs;
- **goes to the zone:** internal taxes, planning, welfare;
- **the boundary does not forgive:** a subsidy is either a loan or an admission
  of failure. A free subsidy destroys the information content: the zone stops
  knowing whether it works if it is being propped up.

### An investment in knowledge

"If it works, we will spread it" is the right goal. But the honest version of
the investment is one sentence: the state publishes the failure too. A cheap
honest failure costs less than a fake success replicated across regions. And
self-sufficiency means handing over an existing territory, not building from
scratch — otherwise the zone depends on outside money from day one and there is
no experiment to speak of.

## 16. Legal form

Common ownership has to sit inside something. The form follows whoever pays.

| Source of money | Form | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The state | municipal property | the state already owns: common ownership rests on the municipal base with no buyout |
| Member-investors | a cooperative or the like | members own without transferable shares, which would reintroduce rent |
| The zone itself | a foundation or mutual | assets held in trust, owned by no one individually |

Three clarifications without which the form lies.

**Municipal property — yes, municipal governance — no.** Municipal property
drags in a council, a mayor and civil-service rules — the representative layer
the model is leaving. So ownership is municipal, while governance is delegated
to the zone's own bodies. Without that separation you have rebuilt a
municipality under a new name.

**"Investors" must be members, not outside capital.** An outside investor
expects a return, and a return is the rent the model abolishes. Money from an
investor who keeps a right to repayment is a loan, bought out of future surplus;
after the buyout, ownership is common. And a member is a resident, not a worker:
membership is by residence, otherwise the person who does not work stops being a
member.

**Form and buyout are linked.** In the municipal case there is no buyout — the
state already owns. In the cooperative case the buyout is built in: a loan or
non-transferable shares repaid out of surplus over decades. The form is the
mechanism of the buyout, not the paperwork after it.

**Compensation is a finite rent, not an asset price.** What is bought out is the
stream of actual income shown in the books, not the market value: the market
price includes the future growth that the commons itself will create. The claim
is paid over a limited number of years and then expires. This is the liquidation
of a permanent claim into a finite one — the principle of "no permanent claim"
carried to its end.

## 17. Consent and membership

A territory is populated by people who did not choose the model. Hence two
questions that must not be conflated: property and membership.

**Property is not negotiable.** Everything that yields rent — land, networks,
enterprises — becomes common for everyone on the territory, whether or not a
person has joined. Partial common ownership is unstable: the private remainder
sucks out the surplus that the commons creates. One cannot "not join" and stay
an owner.

**Membership is voluntary.** Joining or not is a free choice. A member gains a
voice and a dividend; a non-member does not. Both concern only the surplus and
the decisions, not property and not the base.

**The base is in kind, for everyone, unconditionally.** Food, water, heat and
shelter are delivered as a ration, a meter and an assigned dwelling, not bought
with internal currency. Internal money governs the dividend, not survival. So a
dissenter cannot be starved: agreement buys comfort, disagreement buys austerity,
but never hunger. That is the boundary between "one's own currency" and "a
siege".

**No one is expelled or squeezed out.** A home is not a job one quits: on
leaving, a person loses community, the value of their housing, their children's
school. Pressure that forces someone to leave is expulsion without the paperwork.
So exit is a right, not a consequence. A non-member stays a resident, keeps the
state's citizenship, outside money and personal belongings.

**Units neither dictate nor are silenced.** A minority cannot impose a decision
on the majority. But one person's right to say "this decision about me is
wrong", the right of appeal and delay, is not dictatorship but due process. To
remove it is to build the judge of the dissenter from section 9.

**Consent is selection, not suppression.** If the territory does not gather
enough willing people, the answer is not pressure but the admission that the
model has failed its test of consent. A zone is created where people want to
join, not where they are made to.
## 18. What we do not know

An honest list, because the list is the agenda.

- **The labour intensity of the basket** at present technology — the key
  unknown. Until it is calculated, every number above is a skeleton, not an
  answer.
- **The actual return on automation** at the scale of a zone. There is data by
  industry; there is none by territory as a whole.
- **How people behave when hours fall.** Whether they work for a society in
  which they have a voice, or find ways not to work. This is the central
  question of the model, and it is not settled by argument.
- **The dynamics of the norm.** Under tolerance of refusal the norm may either
  strengthen ("this is how it is done here") or erode ("everyone can see it is
  allowed"). The direction is unknown, and the whole arithmetic of section 7
  depends on it.
- **Demography.** An ageing zone has a growing care load and a shrinking
  working-age contour at the same time. The model may be correct and still
  unviable.
- **Who loses, and how badly.** Landowners, employers, importers, part of the
  administration. Without a compensation plan, the first year ends in a
  political conflict rather than a result.
