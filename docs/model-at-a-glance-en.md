# DEKSIRIP in 5 minutes

## The idea in one paragraph

DEKSIRIP is not a replacement for the whole economy. It is a testable model of
local coordination. It tries to connect people with unmet basic needs to unused
common resources through open information, predefined access rights, limited
mandates and independent verification.

## The problem

A resource can exist while a need remains unmet. Five links are often missing:
information, an access rule, a priority decision, an executor and a check on the
result. DEKSIRIP treats this as an organizational problem, not only a shortage
of money or production.

## How it works

```text
need
  ↓
verification of need and access rights
  ↓
visible stocks, capacity, labour, costs and constraints
  ↓
matching options against the physical balance
  ↓
decision by residents and authorized operators
  ↓
limited resources and staged execution
  ↓
independent audit: promise → result → correction
```

Basic goods follow a predefined access rule. For projects beyond the basic
level, goals, timelines, costs, risks and alternatives are published. Support
can be released in stages and stopped when results deteriorate.

## Example

Suppose a neighbourhood has spare kitchen capacity, food in storage and
residents who lack prepared meals. The system does not begin by voting on every
portion:

1. the need is recorded in aggregate form;
2. the store and kitchen publish actual stocks and constraints;
3. basic portions are provided under a pre-agreed rule;
4. spare capacity can be proposed for additional projects;
5. residents compare projects using published costs and expected results;
6. an audit compares promised volume, actual delivery and remaining stock.

If food is insufficient, the model does not promise the impossible. It shows the
shortage, priority, alternatives and the basis for revising the decision.

## What is common and what remains personal

- **Common assets:** selected public or collective resources whose returns are
  distributed under pre-agreed rules.
- **Basic goods:** a minimum set whose access does not depend only on ability to
  pay.
- **Personal property:** housing, belongings, income and assets outside the
  pilot mandate remain personal.
- **Market and external economy:** remain available for trade, comparison and
  exit from the pilot.

## Who decides what

- residents state needs and participate in major changes;
- production teams manage daily operations;
- a coordination layer matches requests, resources and commitments;
- experts build scenarios and state uncertainty;
- auditors check data and outcomes;
- an AI assistant explains information and finds inconsistencies, but does not
  vote or control resources.

The more a decision affects other people and future resources, the stronger the
requirements for publicity, justification and independent review.

## What the model does not promise

It does not eliminate scarcity with one rule, replace professional management
with universal voting or abolish all markets. Its narrower hypothesis is that a
transparent link between need, resource, decision and outcome can improve local
allocation and direct part of the surplus toward development.

## What must be tested

The pilot must show whether access to basic goods improves, whether the gap
between reported and actual resources narrows, whether participation paralyzes
execution, and whether audit, appeal and the right to exit can stop a failing
arrangement.
