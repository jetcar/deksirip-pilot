# DEKSIRIP: High-Level Model Framework

**Status:** Draft 0.1

This document explains the model before technical choices. Its purpose is to
let a researcher understand the proposal without knowing the software
architecture.

## 1. The starting problem

A society can have unused resources while people lack basic goods. A resource
does not connect to a need automatically: access rules, information about
availability, priority decisions, an executor and verification are required.

DEKSIRIP treats this as an institutional coordination problem, not only as a
problem of money or production capacity.

## 2. Main hypothesis

If common resources are made visible, basic needs are separated from purchasing
power, and decisions are tied to measurable consequences and independent audit,
a local economy may direct surplus toward needs and development without a single
centre assigning every production task.

This is a testable hypothesis, not a claim of a proven universal model.

## 3. Five mechanisms

### 3.1. Basic provision

A minimum basket of essential goods is provided as a right of community
participation, not only as a purchase available to people with income. Capacity
limits and shortages are disclosed.

### 3.2. Common share

A distributable share of revenue from common assets is returned to residents in
an equal or otherwise pre-agreed form. It provides security and enables project
choice, but does not give personal ownership of land, factories or infrastructure.

### 3.3. Transparent economic information

Decisions use more than money. Stocks, capacity, labour, energy, materials,
timelines, ecological impacts and alternative uses of resources are visible.

### 3.4. Voluntary direction of surplus

After basic provision, part of the discretionary surplus can support projects.
Projects compete through clear goals, inputs, risks and expected outcomes. Support
is released in stages and can be stopped.

### 3.5. Feedback and audit

Every major decision should leave a verifiable chain:

> need → proposal → rule → resource → action → result.

If the result differs from the promise, the rule or project is reviewed. Errors
cannot be hidden by deleting history.

## 4. How a need connects to a resource

The process is:

1. A resident or group records a need.
2. Producers and common-asset operators publish available resources and limits.
3. Coordination matches the need with stock, capacity and existing commitments.
4. Basic goods use a pre-established access right.
5. Projects publish options, inputs, timelines and alternatives.
6. Residents and authorised bodies decide within the physical balance available.
7. An executor receives a limited mandate and staged resources.
8. Independent review compares the promised and actual result.

This does not mean every request is automatically satisfied. It makes the reason
for refusal, competing options and the possibility of revision visible.

## 5. Decision levels

- **Personal:** use of a personal share and choice to participate.
- **Basic:** food, housing, healthcare, education and other agreed essentials.
- **Project:** selection among additional projects and stages.
- **Strategic:** reserves, major infrastructure and long-term constraints.
- **Emergency:** temporary measures with automatic expiry and audit.

The more a decision affects other people and future resources, the stronger the
requirements for justification, visibility and independent review should be.

## 6. Participant roles

- **Residents** express needs, deliberate and vote, verify outcomes and appeal.
- **Production teams** manage daily work, publish capacity and answer for delivery.
- **The coordination layer** combines requests, constraints and commitments but
  does not receive unlimited control over the economy.
- **Experts** produce calculations and scenarios with stated uncertainty.
- **The AI assistant** explains data and finds contradictions but cannot vote or
  control resources.
- **Auditors** independently check data, decisions and physical outcomes.
- **The external economy** remains available for trade, comparison and a check
  that the internal system has not become an end in itself.

## 7. What the model is not

The model does not promise to eliminate scarcity through one rule, replace
professional management with voting, or abolish every market. Transparency alone
does not remove conflicts of interest. The proposal concerns a bounded set of
common assets and rules that can be tested in a pilot.

## 8. What the pilot tests

The pilot should answer five questions:

1. Does access to basic goods improve without hidden coercion or lower quality?
2. Does surplus move toward projects with clearer public value?
3. Does the gap between reported and actual resources decrease?
4. Can resident participation complement rather than paralyse professional
   execution?
5. Can audit and exit rights stop a failing arrangement?

The registry, electronic voting and AI are instruments for testing this framework,
not the model’s purpose.
