# Pilot Kit: One Allocation Cycle You Can Run Yourself

**Version 1.0 · November 2026 · CC BY 4.0**

## 1. The hypothesis

If a group publishes what it actually has — stocks, capacity, unit costs and
constraints — and connects need, access rule, priority decision, delivery and
audit into one visible cycle, then (a) the gap between recorded and actual
resources shrinks and (b) a larger share of expressed needs is met from the same
resources, without a permanent coordinator.

Both parts are measurable within one cycle. Neither is proven.

## 2. What this does not test

This is the coordination layer only. It does not test:

- unconditional basic provision as a right;
- the social dividend;
- common ownership of productive assets;
- strategic reserves;
- the legal limits of a settlement-level pilot.

Those need a host with legal authority, a budget and an independent evaluation
team, and are described in the [concept note](concept-note-en.md). Do not
describe the result of this cycle as a test of DEKSIRIP as a whole.

## 3. What you need

| Requirement | Minimum | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| A resource pool with real scarcity | one pool, existing records | without scarcity there is nothing to allocate |
| People entitled to it | roughly 15–200 | below that, discrepancy measures are noise |
| Coordinator | 4–6 hours per week | the open question is whether this is enough |
| Auditor | one person who does not execute decisions | otherwise the audit is self-report |
| Records | a spreadsheet and one public page | the cycle is about visible information |
| Time | 2 weeks baseline + 8–12 weeks | shorter cycles do not show staging effects |

If any of the first four is missing, do not run it now. Run it later, or
somewhere else.

## 4. Where it fits

- a housing, consumer or food cooperative with a shared budget, room, vehicle or stock;
- an energy community, or a building with a shared heating or electricity budget;
- a community kitchen, food bank or tool library;
- a student dormitory, co-housing project or shared workspace with bookable capacity;
- a pop-up village or temporary community with its own internal economy;
- a small municipality or neighbourhood association with a discretionary budget;
- a farm or makerspace where equipment time is the scarce resource.

In the European Union, energy communities are already legal entities that own
assets and distribute benefits, so a cycle can be run inside one without any
change in the law.

## 5. Week 0: baseline

Do these five things before anything is announced:

1. **Physical count.** Count what is actually there, by hand, independently of
   the records. One afternoon. This number is the point of the exercise: it
   gives you the discrepancy you will compare against.
2. **Publish the register.** What exists, what is already committed, what is
   free, unit cost, capacity limit, condition. Ugly is fine.
3. **Publish the access rule.** Who is entitled to what, and on what basis.
   Written before requests arrive, not after.
4. **Open an intake form.** Aggregate needs, counted by type, no names at intake.
5. **Write the priority rule.** How you will decide when demand exceeds supply,
   and who decides.

Then record the previous equivalent period: how many needs were expressed, how
many were met, how long a decision took. Your own past is the cheapest
comparison you will ever get.

## 6. Weeks 1–12: the cycle

- **Weeks 1–2 — intake.** Collect requests in aggregate. Publish the list: how
  many of each type, total demand against total available.
- **Week 3 — decision.** Publish the ranked list with the trade-off in plain
  words: this fits, that does not, because of this constraint. No decision
  without a published reason.
- **Weeks 3–10 — staged delivery.** Release in tranches. The next tranche is
  released only if the previous one was delivered as promised. If it was not,
  say so publicly and stop.
- **Week 11 — audit.** Compare promise, delivery and remaining stock. The
  auditor writes first; management responds second.
- **Week 12 — publish.** Discrepancy, six numbers, what broke and where.

One page per week, one public page in total. If maintaining the public page
costs more than the resource being allocated, that is itself a finding.

## 7. The six numbers

Record these throughout. They are the entire dataset.

1. Share of expressed needs met in this cycle.
2. Discrepancy between records and physical count at the end of the cycle,
   absolute and as a percentage.
3. Days from request to decision, and from decision to delivery.
4. Coordinator hours per decision.
5. Decisions reversed, stopped or corrected by the audit.
6. Participation: how many of the entitled used the register or took part in
   the priority step; how many declined or left.

## 8. Comparison and pre-registration

Comparison, in order of cost:

- the same pool in the previous period (weakest, cheapest, always available);
- a similar pool that keeps the old process (better);
- a randomized assignment of pools to old and new process (best; say so if you
  did it, and say so if you did not).

Before week 1, write the six numbers, the comparison and the failure thresholds
into a dated file and publish it. A commit, a gist or an OSF registration is
enough. This is the step that separates a pilot from an anecdote.

## 9. Failure thresholds

Set your own before you start and do not move them afterwards. For example: the
discrepancy falls by less than a third; decision time more than doubles; fewer
than a third of the entitled take part in the priority step; coordination cost
per decision exceeds the value of the resource allocated.

Two or more triggered means the mechanism failed in this setting. That is a
result, not an embarrassment, and it may be the most useful thing you publish.

## 10. Ethical floor

Non-negotiable, even in a small pilot:

- no one's access to an essential is made conditional on taking part;
- exit is free and without penalty;
- no individual medical, financial or political data in the public register —
  aggregates and pseudonyms only;
- the audit is published before management replies;
- if the pool serves essentials and access gets worse, stop. Stopping is part of
  the test, not a failure of it.

## 11. What to publish

One page: the six numbers, the thresholds, the result, what broke and where. A
dated public file is enough. A paper is not required, and neither is the
author's agreement.
