# Risks and Ethics

## Main risks

### Administrative capture

The operator, auditor or data custodian could become a new privileged group.

**Mitigations:** short mandates, rotation, independent audit, public logs,
conflict-of-interest rules and a real replacement procedure.

### Hidden external subsidy

The pilot could appear successful because the surrounding economy supplies
cheap food, credit, energy or skilled labor.

**Mitigations:** publish external transfers, record full opportunity costs and
report results with and without external support.

### Selection bias

Residents who volunteer may be unusually cooperative or wealthy.

**Mitigations:** publish recruitment criteria, report who declined, and compare
the pilot with matched populations.

### Information and privacy abuse

Transparency could expose personal medical, financial or political data.

**Mitigations:** publish aggregate accounts, pseudonymize individual data,
separate public audit from personal records and require due process for access.

### Coercion and loss of exit

A pilot can become a closed social system if people cannot leave or refuse
participation.

**Mitigations:** voluntary entry, a practical exit route, portable personal
assets and uninterrupted access to legal rights and emergency services.

### Crisis failure

Open deliberation may be too slow during a shortage or epidemic.

**Mitigations:** pre-authorized emergency powers with narrow scope, expiry
dates, published triggers, after-action review and independent investigation.

## Ethical threshold

No pilot should proceed if participation is coerced, basic services are made
conditional on political loyalty, or residents cannot challenge decisions and
leave without disproportionate loss.
